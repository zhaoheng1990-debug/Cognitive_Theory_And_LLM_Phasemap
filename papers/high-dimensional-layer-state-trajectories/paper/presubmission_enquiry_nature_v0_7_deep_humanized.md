## Manuscript-comments box

Dear Editors,

We seek advice on whether our Article, "Ordered layer-state trajectories enable analysis of transformer inference", would be suitable for *Nature*. Mechanistic interpretability can localize information to parameters, heads, neurons and circuits. It has not supplied a single empirical object for following the ordered course of one prompted inference. Our Article uses the prompt-conditioned sequence of high-dimensional layer states for that purpose.

Across representative Qwen, Llama and Gemma checkpoints, direct hidden states provided stronger mechanism coordinates than vocabulary readbacks, and realized order was 1.60-2.24 times more chord-aligned than fixed-endpoint shuffles. The signature recurred in controlled lexical, arithmetic and multiple-choice tasks. Random initializations showed larger alignment, whereas reordering trained blocks changed outputs. Component-swap controls separated the architecture-compatible propagation scaffold from training-organized coordinates and functionally consequential execution. A confidence-gated local-transition actuator crossed a declared full-vocabulary candidate boundary in 17/87, 13/87 and 9/87 held-out relation-graph prompts, with 0/203, 2/203 and 0/203 non-target changes. The Article also reports failed additive interventions, a non-confirmatory mixed-arithmetic transfer test and a corrected direction-specificity null.

The evidence supports a process-level experimental design: select an observable process, audit its coordinates, compare realized order with a matched null, then perturb a declared transition or output boundary. We present this as a methodological question for distributed systems with measurable states and legitimate interventions. It does not assume shared mechanisms across artificial, biological and physical systems. Processed Source Data, extraction metadata, code and null results are available through the cited Zenodo concept records and the paper-specific GitHub branch. We would value your assessment of the Article's suitability for *Nature*.

## Abstract box

An experiment that follows extended computation in a language model needs a representation of the process as it unfolds, alongside measurements of individual components¹–⁴. We follow each inference as an ordered trajectory of high-dimensional layer states. Across representative Qwen, Llama and Gemma checkpoints, direct hidden states provided stronger mechanism coordinates than vocabulary readbacks, while native layer order was 1.60-2.24 times more chord-aligned than endpoint-preserving shuffles. The signature recurred in controlled lexical, arithmetic and multiple-choice tasks. Random initialization increased the effect, identifying an architecture-compatible propagation scaffold. Reordering trained blocks still changed outputs, separating this scaffold from trained, functionally consequential organization. A confidence-gated local-transition actuator switched the emitted top token across a predeclared conflict-to-clean boundary in 17/87, 13/87 and 9/87 held-out relation-graph prompts, with 0/203, 2/203 and 0/203 non-target output changes. These trajectories support process-level observation, comparison and selective perturbation of transformer inference. The output result applies to this controlled task family; it does not establish correctness control or a general predictive law.
